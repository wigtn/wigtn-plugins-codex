# Swiss Minimal

Use when rigorous information hierarchy and alignment matter more than visual
novelty. This direction succeeds through proportion, not through empty screens.

## Direction contract

- Build on a visible 8- or 12-column grid with a consistent baseline rhythm.
  Align headings, labels, media, and actions to shared edges.
- Use one neutral sans-serif family with 3–4 weights. Favor sentence case and
  weight/size contrast over letter spacing or decorative type.
- Establish a compact modular scale such as 12, 14, 16, 20, 28, 40, 56.
  Body text stays at least 16px on reading surfaces.
- Use white or near-white surfaces, near-black ink, quiet gray metadata, and one
  saturated accent. The accent marks priority and state, not decoration.
- Prefer square or subtly rounded geometry, 1px rules, and whitespace. Remove a
  container when grouping remains clear without it.
- Make navigation typographic and predictable. Active state needs more than a
  color shift: weight, rule, background, or an accessible current-page marker.
- Let photography use deliberate crops and strong rectangles. Keep icon style
  consistent and secondary to labels.

## Interaction

- Use 120–200ms transitions for state clarification only.
- Preserve layout during loading. Avoid motion that changes reading order.
- Provide high-contrast focus rings, 44px touch targets where possible, and
  complete keyboard navigation.

## Avoid

- Arbitrary asymmetry presented as “Swiss,” oversized empty heroes, or grids
  that collapse into unrelated spacing values.
- Gray-on-gray body copy, all-uppercase paragraphs, pill-shaped everything, and
  decorative gradients.
- Hiding necessary instructions in tooltips to preserve visual cleanliness.

## Done when

Every major edge can be explained by the grid, hierarchy survives grayscale,
the narrow layout preserves reading order, and removing the accent color does
not remove meaning.
