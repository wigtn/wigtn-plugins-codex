# Editorial

Use for reading-led products where hierarchy, pacing, and imagery carry more
meaning than interface chrome. Extend the project’s type and color tokens first.

## Direction contract

- Lead with a strong headline, a short deck, byline or metadata, and a clear
  reading column. Make the opening hierarchy obvious without decoration.
- Pair one expressive display face with a quiet text face only when the project
  already supports both. Keep controls in the text face.
- Aim for 55–75 characters per body line, 1.5–1.75 line height, and visibly
  larger gaps between sections than between related paragraphs.
- Use a 12-column grid for landing pages and a 6–8 column content grid for
  articles. Break the grid deliberately for pull quotes or lead media, not for
  every card.
- Keep the palette paper-like and restrained: one ink, one muted text color,
  one surface, and one editorial accent with accessible contrast.
- Treat captions, source notes, dates, and categories as real information.
  Give them a consistent smaller scale instead of reducing opacity at random.
- Prefer rules, whitespace, and alignment over rounded containers. Cards are
  acceptable for distinct tools or related stories, not for every paragraph.
- Crop images intentionally and preserve a meaningful focal point. Always set
  dimensions and useful alt text.

## Interaction

- Underline links or give them another persistent non-color cue.
- Use restrained fades or vertical reveals under 240ms. Reading must remain
  stable when motion is disabled.
- Keep sticky elements small and prevent them from narrowing the reading column.
- Provide visible keyboard focus and a skip link on long pages.

## Avoid

- Magazine styling on dense operational screens.
- Centering long text, excessive italics, tiny low-contrast captions, or more
  than two font families.
- Filling every empty area with a quote, image, badge, or decorative serif.
- Simulated print effects that reduce legibility or performance.

## Done when

The first viewport communicates story, author/context, and next reading action;
body text remains comfortable at 320px; headings form a coherent outline; and
the page still works with images, custom fonts, and motion unavailable.
