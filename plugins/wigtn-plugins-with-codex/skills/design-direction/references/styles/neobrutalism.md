# Neobrutalism

Use for bold consumer, cultural, or experimental products that can support high
visual energy. Preserve task clarity beneath the expressive surface.

## Direction contract

- Establish a strict border token, usually 2–4px, and one hard-offset shadow
  system. Consistency makes the deliberate roughness credible.
- Use a small high-contrast palette: neutral canvas/ink plus 2–3 flat accents.
  Every text/background pair still needs accessible contrast.
- Choose a sturdy display face and a readable UI/body face, or one family with
  strong weights. Reserve uppercase and compressed type for short labels.
- Use blocky geometry, explicit section boundaries, and slightly irregular
  composition within a real grid.
- Buttons should look pressable: border, offset shadow, and a pressed state that
  moves toward the shadow. Disabled state must remain legible.
- Cards need distinct content roles. Do not add borders and shadows to nested
  elements until hierarchy becomes noisy.
- Illustrations may be flat and imperfect, but icons and operational controls
  remain recognizable.

## Interaction

- Use direct 80–160ms press, nudge, or color-swap feedback.
- Provide a conventional focus ring that is distinct from the decorative border.
- Avoid large moving shadows or perpetual jitter. Respect reduced motion.
- Confirm destructive actions plainly; visual playfulness must not soften risk.

## Avoid

- Random border widths, random rotations, rainbow palettes, and novelty cursor
  replacements.
- Long all-caps copy or expressive fonts in forms and tables.
- Confusing decorative misalignment with broken layout.
- Applying the style to regulated, safety-critical, or high-density workflows
  without a strong product reason.

## Done when

The system can be described with a small set of border, shadow, color, and type
tokens; primary tasks are obvious; keyboard focus is unambiguous; and the layout
survives long text and narrow screens.
